I started programming in 1991 as a hobby — long before it became a career. My early years were spent with Turbo Pascal, C, C++, Java, SQL, and Visual Basic, building compilers, operating systems, and small applications just to understand how things worked.

In 1992 I enrolled in Computer Science at the University of Salerno and graduated with honours in 1998. A brief period of military service followed, and in 1999 I joined a well-known American software company in Rome — where I still work today.

Over 25+ years in that company I have worked across every layer of the software stack: from low-level systems programming to distributed cloud platforms, from monolithic architectures to microservices, from early J2EE applications to modern AI-powered systems.

Today I work in the Cloud area, designing and implementing resilient, cloud-native solutions. My day-to-day spans architecture decisions, platform engineering, and applied AI — bridging the gap between cutting-edge research and production-ready systems.

I have lived through every major wave of the software industry:

- Procedural → Object-Oriented → Component-based
- Monoliths → SOA → Microservices
- On-premise → Virtualisation → Cloud-Native
- Rule-based AI → Machine Learning → Large Language Models and Agentic AI

That perspective shapes everything I write. Not just *how* to use a technology, but *why* it exists, *what problem* it was designed to solve, and *where* it is heading.

Code4Projects is my attempt to share that depth — not tutorials copied from the docs, but genuine understanding from someone who has actually worked on these systems.

I also aim to offer **strategic guidance**: helping engineers and organisations understand which technologies matter, which are hype, and how to make sound decisions in a landscape that never stops changing.
